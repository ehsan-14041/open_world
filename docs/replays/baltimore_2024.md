**Why this event was chosen**

- BENCHMARK #2, chosen to test whether the Yantian failure pattern is a property of the MODEL or of the Yantian proxy. It differs from Yantian on every dimension that could have caused a spurious result: different year (2024 vs 2021), different continent, different trigger (physical channel blockage vs COVID labour absence), and critically OUTSIDE the 2021 global congestion wave that confounded the Yantian observations.
- The capacity loss is near-total and unambiguous — the navigation channel was physically blocked — so the injected shock needs no estimation of an operating percentage.
- The restoration timeline is documented to the day by a public academic reference source, which gives DATED MILESTONES even though no weekly throughput series exists.
- Duration (~11 weeks to full channel reopening) is long enough to resolve recovery dynamics at weekly resolution, and is about twice Yantian's, so a 'recovery too fast' error should be easier to see.
- Chosen despite a known weakness: the disrupted port's own monthly throughput could not be obtained publicly. That weakness is itself part of the finding.

> DESIGN DECISION, and the point of this replay: only the INITIATING shock is injected, for a single week. The phased channel restoration is deliberately NOT injected. Everything after week 1 — including the entire recovery path of port_capacity — is a pure model OUTPUT, so the observed restoration milestones are a genuine falsification test of the model's recovery dynamics rather than a check on an input we supplied. This is the opposite of the Yantian setup, where the capacity path was injected in phases and therefore untestable.

**Variables with no observations** (not evaluated)

- `port_capacity` — No weekly or monthly TEU series for the Port of Baltimore during March-August 2024 was obtainable publicly. Notably, the NEIGHBOURING ports' monthly volumes (NY/NJ, Virginia) were published promptly — observability is asymmetric, and the disrupted node is the hardest one to see.
- `shipping_delay` — No port-local transit-delay series exists. The global Sea-Intelligence series used for Yantian was deliberately NOT reused here: applying the same discredited global proxy to a second event would reproduce the first event's confounding rather than test the model independently.
- `order_backlog` — Diverted volumes at NY/NJ (790,758 TEU in May 2024) and Virginia (>340,000 TEU in May 2024) were measured and published, but neither port authority quantified how much was Baltimore diversion, and the model's order_backlog is an index against a normal open order book that has no published counterpart.
- `freight_cost` — No continuous public weekly lane rate series obtainable, as for Yantian.
- `inventory_availability` — Latent: firm-internal, no public counterpart.
- `production_capacity` — Latent: firm-internal, no public counterpart attributable to this port.
- `service_level` — Latent: firm-internal. The model's headline outcome remains unobservable in a second, entirely independent event — which is now a pattern rather than an accident of one case.
- `consumer_price_pressure` — Operates at national/quarterly resolution; not attributable to one port inside a weekly window.

## Limitations

- One episode only: calibration and evaluation share the same window, so nothing here is out-of-sample validation.
- The only scoreable series is a **global monthly aggregate** standing in for a **single-port weekly** variable. See mapping `map_delay_from_si_global`.
- Mid-2021 container shipping was disrupted by many simultaneous causes; movement in the observed series cannot be attributed to this port.
- Six of the model's eight variables have no observations at all, including the headline outcome `service_level`.
- The injected event's magnitude comes from reported operating-capacity percentages, not measured throughput.
