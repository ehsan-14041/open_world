**Why this event was chosen**

- It is a CONTAINER PORT CAPACITY disruption, which is exactly what world_models/supply_chain/port_disruption.json models. A canal blockage (e.g. Suez 2021) would have required modelling a different mechanism.
- The reported capacity loss (to ~30% of normal, i.e. -70 index points) coincidentally matches the magnitude of the simulator's default scenario, so the replay tests the model in the regime it was designed for.
- The duration (~4.5 weeks) is long enough to be resolved by weekly simulation, unlike the six-day Suez blockage.
- The timeline is documented in publicly readable trade sources with dates.
- No classified or proprietary data is required to define the event.
- Chosen on data availability, not on prominence: Suez 2021 is the more famous event but is too short for weekly resolution and disrupts a canal rather than a port.

## Limitations

- One episode only: calibration and evaluation share the same window, so nothing here is out-of-sample validation.
- The only scoreable series is a **global monthly aggregate** standing in for a **single-port weekly** variable. See mapping `map_delay_from_si_global`.
- Mid-2021 container shipping was disrupted by many simultaneous causes; movement in the observed series cannot be attributed to this port.
- Six of the model's eight variables have no observations at all, including the headline outcome `service_level`.
- The injected event's magnitude comes from reported operating-capacity percentages, not measured throughput.
