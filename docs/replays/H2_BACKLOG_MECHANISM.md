# H2 mechanism test — does a real order backlog behave like a stock?

> Generated by `python scripts/test_queue_mechanism.py --h2 --write-report`. **No simulation is involved.** No coefficient was changed.

## Why H2 needs a different test from H1

[H1 was supported](H1_QUEUE_MECHANISM.md) by an increment-shape argument: that queue had a clean build phase under a flat driver, and relaxation is *structurally* barred from producing non-decaying increments under a steady driver.

A backlog gives no such clean phase — its driver moves throughout, so shape alone is ambiguous. The distinctive signature of a stock is instead **path dependence**:

| Form | Prediction |
|---|---|
| Relaxation | backlog is a function of CURRENT pressure — same pressure, same backlog |
| Stock (H2) | backlog is the INTEGRAL of past imbalances — same pressure, different backlog depending on the path |

So the decisive test is hysteresis, and the prediction was stated before testing: **positive hysteresis gap: detrended backlog cover on the down-leg exceeds the up-leg at matched capacity utilisation**.

## Two traps this test had to avoid

**1. Circularity.**

> US Census M3 DERIVES New Orders as Shipments + change in Unfilled Orders. New Orders is therefore NOT an independent driver for this test and is deliberately excluded. Capacity utilisation is measured separately by the Federal Reserve (G.17) and is used as the pressure variable instead.

Verified empirically: the identity holds to about 1.1% median relative error, so New Orders is effectively a derived quantity. Federal Reserve capacity utilisation is used as the pressure variable instead — measured by a different agency, with no accounting relationship to unfilled orders.

**2. Trend masquerading as hysteresis.** Backlog cover trends upward across the window, so an uncontrolled matched-pressure comparison across years measures the trend rather than path dependence. Cover is linearly detrended and the comparison restricted to a single capacity-utilisation cycle.

## Data — US manufacturing unfilled orders excluding transportation, 2018-2024

Monthly unfilled orders (backlog) and value of shipments for US manufacturing EXCLUDING transportation equipment, with Federal Reserve manufacturing capacity utilisation as an independently measured pressure variable.

US Census M3 and Federal Reserve G.17 series, retrieved via FRED. **Public domain, so the series are stored in this repository** — the first fully redistributable data in the project.

## Result 1 — hysteresis (detrended, within one cycle)

Cycle: pressure trough **2020-04** (62.5%) → peak **2021-11** (78.4%). Secular trend removed: +0.00339 months of cover per month.

| Capacity utilisation | Up-leg (detrended cover) | Down-leg | Gap | Supports H2? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 76.5–77.5% | +0.0324 (n=3) | +0.0177 (n=8) | **-0.0147** | **no** |
| 77.5–78.5% | +0.0269 (n=6) | -0.0022 (n=11) | **-0.0290** | **no** |

Mean gap **-0.0219** — the wrong sign. At matched capacity utilisation, backlog cover was *lower* on the way down than on the way up, which is the opposite of what a stock predicts.

## Result 2 — the same question without trend control

Pressure returned to its starting level (77.9% in 2018-01 → 78.3% in 2021-12), and backlog cover over the same span went **0.837 → 1.070** (×1.28).

Taken at face value that is a **+28% persistent backlog** and looks like strong support for H2. It is not: this comparison is not detrended, and the entire gap is the secular trend that the controlled test removes.

**The two tests disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.** The uncontrolled version would have promoted H2 on an artefact.

## Verdict

### H2 NOT SUPPORTED

at matched capacity utilisation, detrended backlog cover is LOWER on the down-leg than the up-leg — the opposite of what a stock predicts. Note the test is underpowered: national aggregates average together industries that are accumulating and industries that are draining.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does establish:** in national aggregate manufacturing data, an order backlog does not show the path dependence a stock predicts, once the secular trend is removed.

**Does not establish that H2 is wrong.** The test is *underpowered for the claim*: the model's `order_backlog` is a node-level, disruption-specific backlog, while this series aggregates every US manufacturing industry — averaging together sectors that are accumulating and sectors that are draining. That is precisely the kind of averaging that would hide a node-level stock.

> A negative result here is NOT a refutation of H2 for a single disrupted node. It says the predicted signature is absent in national aggregate data, which is the wrong resolution for a node-level claim. H2 should be retested if a node-level backlog series can ever be sourced — not deleted on this evidence.

**H2 is therefore marked `not_supported`, not `rejected`, and is not deleted.**

## A definitional problem this test exposed in the model

`order_backlog` is currently described as *"unfulfilled orders queued behind the disruption, including cargo waiting at anchor"* — which conflates two different things with different dynamics and different observability:

| Thing | Dynamics | Observability | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cargo physically waiting at the port | integrates (H1) | proxy-observable | **supported** |
| Unfulfilled customer orders in the order book | not shown to integrate (H2) | latent at node level | **not supported** |

One variable cannot carry both. Splitting them is a candidate structural change in its own right — and note it would be a change made for a *definitional* reason, not to improve a fit.

## Limitations

- NATIONAL AGGREGATE. The model's order_backlog is a node-level, disruption-specific backlog; aggregating across all industries averages together sectors that are accumulating and sectors that are draining, which attenuates exactly the dynamic under test. This test is therefore UNDERPOWERED for a node-level claim.
- Backlog cover (backlog / shipments) has a secular upward trend across the window; any matched-pressure comparison must be detrended or it measures trend rather than hysteresis.
- The April 2020 cover spike is a denominator artefact: shipments fell about 19% while backlog was flat, so cover rose without any accumulation.
- One capacity-utilisation cycle is available in the window, giving small matched-pressure samples.

## Consequence for the roadmap

1. H1 — **independently supported**, may proceed to experimental implementation.
2. H2 — **not supported at this resolution**, does not proceed, is not deleted.
3. The topology sketch `queue_stock → delay → backlog_stock → inventory → service` is **half-supported**: the queue leg has independent evidence, the backlog leg does not. Building both at once would smuggle an untested mechanism in behind a tested one.
4. To test H2 properly, a node-level backlog series is needed — the same procurement problem as [event #3](EVENT3_SEARCH.md), and the same class of customer data that would ground the model's latent downstream half.
