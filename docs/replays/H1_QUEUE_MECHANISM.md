# H1 mechanism test — does a real port queue behave like a stock?

> Generated by `python scripts/test_queue_mechanism.py --write-report`. **No simulation is involved.** The Event Simulator is not imported, no world is run, and no coefficient was changed.

## Why test a mechanism instead of running a third replay

Two historical replays established that the model recovers too early ([cross-event diagnosis](CROSS_EVENT_DIAGNOSIS.md)), and a third event with the required local time series could not be sourced ([event #3 search](EVENT3_SEARCH.md)). But the leading explanation — H1, that a queue is a **stock** that integrates an imbalance rather than a variable that relaxes toward pressure — is a claim about how the real world works. It can be tested directly, on independent data, without any simulation at all.

That is a cheaper and stronger test: if H1 fails here, it should be deleted rather than carried forward into an expensive held-out experiment.

## The two candidate forms

| Form | Law | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Relaxation | `q(t+1) = q(t) + r·(P(t) − q(t))` | what the engine does today |
| Stock (H1) | `q(t+1) = q(t) + (A(t) − C)`, clipped at 0 | the hypothesis |

They are separable **from the shape of the queue alone**: under a steady driver, relaxation closes a fixed fraction of a shrinking gap each period, so its increments must decay geometrically. A stock with a steady imbalance grows linearly.

## Data — Container ships at anchor, San Pedro Bay (Los Angeles / Long Beach), June-October 2021

Container ships at anchor in San Pedro Bay, monthly. Single source, single definition across all five points.

| Month | Ships at anchor | Increment |
|---|---|---|
| 2021-06 | 9 | — |
| 2021-07 | 18 | +9 |
| 2021-08 | 29 | +11 |
| 2021-09 | 40 | +11 |
| 2021-10 | 61 | +21 |

Single source, single measurement definition. Points reported elsewhere under other definitions were **excluded rather than spliced**:

- **73** (2021-09-19) — Different definition — 'container ships in the queue in San Pedro Bay' counts vessels at anchor AND in drift zones, so it is not comparable with the at-anchor series. Splicing the two would manufacture a jump that did not occur.
- **109** (2022-01-09) — The all-time peak, but recorded under the post-November-2021 queuing regime in which ships were required to wait far offshore. The same source notes only 12 vessels were loitering or anchored off the ports that day. The measurement definition changed mid-series; including this point would confound a regime change with a dynamic.
- **0** (2022-11-22) — End of the backlog, under the 'in queue' definition. Retained as context for the drain phase but not mixed into the at-anchor series.

## Result 1 — shape (no fitting, no parameters)

Increments: **+9, +11, +11, +21** — non-decaying, in fact growing.

> increments do not decay — INCONSISTENT with relaxation toward a steady driver

Relaxation closes a fixed fraction of the remaining gap each period, so with a steady driver its increments must shrink geometrically. Increments that hold or grow require the driver itself to be rising.

## Result 2 — fit comparison

| Form | Free parameters | Fitted | Predicted | SSE | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relaxation | 2 (r, P) | r=0.15, P=92 | [9.0, 21.4, 31.9, 40.8, 48.4] | 178.4 | 5.97 |
| Stock | 1 (k) | k=13.0/month | [9.0, 22.0, 35.0, 48.0, 61.0] | 116.0 | 4.82 |

Relaxation's error is **1.54× the stock form's, with one more free parameter**. Its best fit necessarily undershoots the late points, because it must decelerate as the queue approaches its fitted driver.

## Result 3 — the implied driver (the decisive check)

This is the strongest part of the test, because it requires no fit at all — only arithmetic and the public record. Inverting the relaxation law gives the driver that would have had to exist:

| Assumed response rate | Implied driver growth over Jun–Oct 2021 |
|---|---|
| r=0.2 | **~2.7×** |
| r=0.5 | **~3.0×** |

**What the driver actually did:** Port of Los Angeles loaded import volume was flat to declining across this window, not rising.

| Month | Loaded import TEU |
|---|---|
| 2021-05 | 535,714 |
| 2021-06 | 467,763 |
| 2021-08 | 485,672 |
| 2021-10 | 467,287 |
| 2021-11 | 403,444 |

> **Provenance caveat:** These figures were reported by search results as coming from Port of Los Angeles monthly cargo releases. portoflosangeles.org returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so the primary pages could NOT be read directly and these numbers are held to a lower provenance tier than the queue series. They are used only for a qualitative claim (flat vs several-fold growth), which is robust to their exact values: the test's conclusion would be unchanged if every figure were wrong by 20%.

So relaxation requires a roughly threefold rise in the driver over exactly the months when the driver was flat. **That is not a close call, and it does not depend on the exact figures.**

## Verdict

### H1 SUPPORTED

the observed queue rises with non-decaying increments, which relaxation toward a steady driver cannot produce, and the one-parameter stock form fits better.

The verdict rule is deliberately conservative: `H1 SUPPORTED` requires **both** that the stock form fits better **and** that the shape is structurally incompatible with relaxation. A better fit alone would not be enough — an extra parameter can always buy some fit, and here the stock form has *fewer* parameters, not more.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does establish:** a real port queue, measured independently of this project, accumulates like a stock. H1 is no longer merely plausible — it is consistent with observed behaviour, and its rival is not.

**Does not establish:** that adding a queue stock will fix the model's timing bias. That is a different claim and still requires calibrating on one event and evaluating on a **held-out** event, which cannot begin until a third event exists.

The test also cannot speak to **hysteresis**. The drain phase was measured under a different definition after the queuing regime changed in November 2021, so onset and recovery cannot be compared on a consistent basis here.

## Limitations

- Five monthly points. Enough to test the SHAPE of accumulation, not to estimate a drain rate.
- The series covers the BUILD phase only. The drain phase is measured under a different definition after the queuing system changed in November 2021, so onset/recovery asymmetry (hysteresis) cannot be tested on a consistent basis.
- The shock here is demand-side, not a capacity loss. This tests whether a port queue INTEGRATES an imbalance; it does not test the port_disruption model's own event path.
- The driver figures are attributed but not directly verified (see driver_context).
