What happens

Cash in the bank over the next 90 days

Costs take about a week to rise — you are still using stock bought at the old price. Customers take a couple of weeks to react to a new price. Both lags are in the model.

How this chart is made. The model simulates three things: your ingredient cost per order, your average price, and your daily orders. Cash is then calculated from those simulated figures using the business inputs you gave — revenue is orders × price, gross profit is revenue minus ingredient cost, and cash is yesterday's cash plus gross profit minus your fixed costs. Cash is not simulated directly.

Evidence & assumptions

Where every number comes from

Four kinds of number. Yours, which we take as given. External research, with a citation. Our model assumptions, stated as such and tested. And results calculated from the three.

On price sensitivity: external research provides a plausible reference range for eating out as a whole; your actual customers may be more or less price-sensitive than that. The high setting is a stress assumption, not a research finding.

How this was calculated

What this does

  • Compares three responses under one consistent model of your cafe.
  • Shows the relative financial trade-offs between them.
  • Tests how sensitive the result is to each uncertain assumption.
  • Tells you which unknowns matter most — and which don't.

What this does not do

  • Predict the future or guarantee any sales or profit figure.
  • Model how competitors will react to your prices.
  • Model the wider economy, seasons, or new customers.
  • Replace your accountant or financial advice.
